03

PHONE-TO-VEHICLE ROLL/PITCH ALIGNMENT

Gravity-Based Leveling (Phase 3)

ALIGNMENT METHOD

Gravity-based leveling via Rodrigues minimal rotation

The current Phase 3 implementation performs ONLY phone-to-vehicle roll and pitch alignment. By averaging the static acceleration vector, the smartphone's coordinate frame is rotated such that its Z-axis aligns with true gravity.

CURRENT LIMITATION

Absolute Yaw is NOT Solved

  • Why gravity isn't enough: Gravity only provides a 2D vertical reference (pitch and roll). Yaw exists in the orthogonal horizontal plane and cannot be observed from a static gravity vector alone.
  • Why other sensors fail here: Magnetometers suffer from severe hard-iron/soft-iron distortions inside a metal vehicle chassis. Standard GNSS or motion-based yaw estimation can be highly unreliable due to urban canyons and vehicle dynamics.

OBSERVATION & QUANTITATIVE FINDING

Coordinate alignment alone does not eliminate long-term inertial drift.

Phase 3 successfully levels the smartphone's gravity vector to machine precision (det R = 1.000000). However, because consumer MEMS accelerometers carry residual in-run biases (~0.05 m/s²), rotating the sensor frame without a velocity constraint (NHC or AI Speed) still leads to quadratic divergence.

ENGINEERING INSIGHTS & CONCLUSION

Summary of Phase 1–3 Baseline Findings

INSIGHT 1

Classical smartphone INS accumulates severe drift.

Integrating consumer-grade IMU measurements over time produces rapidly growing position error because of sensor noise and bias. Double-integration without external updates causes the trajectory to quickly diverge from ground truth.

INSIGHT 2

Phone orientation matters.

The smartphone's mounting orientation differs from the vehicle reference frame. Sensor measurements exist in the device frame, making phone-to-vehicle alignment an essential prerequisite before interpreting forward or lateral acceleration.

INSIGHT 3

Roll/pitch alignment alone is insufficient.

Comparing Phase 2 and Phase 3 reveals that while S-Vta1a showed a small improvement, S-Vta2 exhibited a regression. This explicitly demonstrates that mathematically leveling gravity does not eliminate accumulated inertial drift in the horizontal plane.

INSIGHT 4

Additional constraints & sensor fusion are required.

The current baseline motivates future work. As a planned next step, the next stage of the system will involve AI-based motion and speed estimation, non-holonomic vehicle constraints, sensor fusion, and map information to bound error.

ENGINEERING CONCLUSION

The current Phase 1–3 implementation establishes a measurable classical INS baseline and demonstrates the limitations of smartphone-only inertial dead reckoning. Roll/pitch alignment addresses sensor-frame orientation but does not remove accumulated inertial drift. The results motivate the next stages of IDR-NAV involving intelligent estimation, sensor fusion, vehicle constraints, and map information.
